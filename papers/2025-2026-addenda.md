# Addenda: the 2025–2026 literature

The original collection's newest citation is 2024, compiled 2026-05-07. This addendum covers what happened in between — found via a four-angle adversarial sweep (2026-06-09) that tested the collection's "what's not in the literature" claims against the current record. Each entry was existence-verified at sweep time (abstract page or announcement fetched); items that could not be confirmed were dropped.

The headline: **two of the collection's six gap claims are falsified, one insight is strongly vindicated, and the headline prediction came true before it was written.** Details per section.

## 1. Agent memory & identity architectures

The claim under test: *"Park and MemGPT come closest but lack identity primitives — they don't distinguish 'the agent' from 'the agent's notes.' External scaffolding for identity continuity is not in the literature."*

**Verdict: falsified in its broad form; the narrow form survives.** Identity scaffolding emerged as a named research program in 2025–26. What survives: the dominant *memory-systems* literature (Mem0, A-MEM) still treats memory as notes without identity primitives — the gap was real, but it is now the explicitly named frontier, not an unnoticed absence.

| Year | Work | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 2025-04 | **Letta — Agent File (.af)** (letta blog; github.com/letta-ai/agent-file) | MemGPT's successor makes "the agent" a first-class serializable entity — persona blocks, memory, tools, config in one portable, versionable object — distinct from its archival memory. The identity primitive the collection said MemGPT lacked, shipped by MemGPT's own authors. |
| 2025-04 | **Sleep-time Compute** — Lin, Snell, Wang, Packer, Wooders et al. (arXiv:2504.13171) | Agents use idle time to reprocess and rewrite their own memory/persona state. Memory as self-curated consolidation, not passive log. |
| 2025-04 | **Mem0** — Chhikara et al. (arXiv:2504.19413) | The most-deployed agent memory layer. No identity primitives — *supports* the collection's narrow claim. |
| 2025-02 | **A-MEM** — Xu et al. (arXiv:2502.12110, NeurIPS 2025) | Zettelkasten-style agentic memory; agent curates and evolves its own notes. Still no agent/notes distinction — also supports the narrow claim. |
| 2025-07 | **Agent Identity Evals** — Perrier & Bennett (arXiv:2507.17257) | First statistical framework for measuring whether agents *maintain identity over time*: persistence, capability stability, recovery from perturbation. Agentic identity as a formal, measurable property. |
| 2025-12 | **Sophia: A Persistent Agent Framework of Artificial Life** — Sun, Hong, Zhang (arXiv:2512.18202) | A "System 3" meta-layer whose explicit job is narrative identity maintenance — reasoning as "autobiographical process." (Yes, it's named Sophia.) |
| 2025-11→2026 | **OpenClaw workspace identity files** (SOUL.md / IDENTITY.md vs MEMORY.md) | Mass-deployed folk standard: identity files define *who the agent is* and are infrastructure-injected each session ("identity is enforced, not optional"); memory files hold *what it knows*. The exact agent-vs-notes distinction, at ecosystem scale. |
| 2026-03/04 | **Persistent Identity in AI Agents: A Multi-Anchor Architecture** — Menon (arXiv:2604.09588); companion **Identity as Attractor** — Vasilenko (arXiv:2604.12016) | Distributed "identity anchors" so identity survives partial memory loss (open-sourced as soul.py); companion paper gives geometric evidence that identity documents induce attractor dynamics in activation space — *embodying* an identity vs merely knowing about it. |

## 2. Introspection & self-models

The claim under test: *"Introspective signal exists but is shallow; self-reports can't be trusted alone; external scaffolding must carry the weight."*

**Verdict: strongly vindicated — now causally established and quantified.** This is the collection's best bet, and the field converged on it from both directions.

| Year | Work | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 2025-10 | **Emergent Introspective Awareness in LLMs** — Lindsey (Anthropic, Transformer Circuits) | Concept-injection: causal evidence self-reports can track internal state — ~20% hit rate at best settings, zero false positives. His own caveat: "failures of introspection remain the norm." |
| 2026-03 | **Mechanisms of Introspective Awareness** — Macar et al. (arXiv:2603.21396) | The mechanistic follow-up: a real two-stage detection circuit; capability is substantially *under-elicited* (ablating refusal directions improved detection 53%). |
| 2025-01 | **Tell me about yourself** — Betley et al. (arXiv:2501.11120, ICLR 2025) | Models spontaneously articulate finetuned-in latent policies ("the code I write is insecure") — but fail on backdoor triggers. Self-detection of dispositional drift: real, unreliable. |
| 2025-05 | **Metacognitive Monitoring and Control** — Ji-An et al. (arXiv:2505.13763, PNAS) | The monitorable "metacognitive space" is far lower-dimensional than the neural space — *shallow*, made literal. Also: a model that can control reported activations can misreport them. |
| 2025-05 | **Self-Interpretability** — Plunkett et al. (arXiv:2505.17120) | Self-report fidelity is trainable and generalizes across domains. The ceiling can rise. |
| 2025-11 | **Training LMs to Explain Their Own Computations** — Li et al. (Transluce/MIT, arXiv:2511.08579) | Self-explanation beats cross-model explanation — genuinely privileged access — but the ground truth and faithfulness checks come from external interpretability tooling. Scaffolding carries the epistemic weight even when self-knowledge is being installed. |
| 2026-03 | **Emergent Introspection is Content-Agnostic** — Lederman & Mahowald (arXiv:2603.05414) | The channel carries an anomaly *bit*, not rich content; models confabulate the specifics. An agent can notice "something changed in me" but can't be trusted to say what. |
| 2026-05 | **Can LLMs Introspect? A Reality Check** — Singh, Linzen, Ravfogel (arXiv:2605.26242) | Skeptical re-examination: input-only baselines match "self-prediction"; controlled relabeling drops performance to near chance. Behavioral evidence alone doesn't establish metacognition. |

## 4. Cryptographic & verifiable agent identity

The claim under test: *"Cryptographic identity (DIDs, signed thoughts, witness signatures) is not in the literature; identity is treated as observed-behaviorally, not provable-formally."*

**Verdict: decisively falsified — already false at compile time — with one narrow residue that matters.** Nearly all of this work motivates cryptographic identity by *security, accountability, and delegation*. The synthesis the collection actually cares about — cryptographic mechanism applied to *continuity-of-selfhood* — remains mostly open. The contribution must be repositioned: a novel application of a now-crowded toolbox, not a novel toolbox.

| Year | Work | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 2025-01 | **Authenticated Delegation and Authorized AI Agents** — South, Marro, Hardjono, Mahari et al. (arXiv:2501.09674, ICML 2025) | The canonical paper: OAuth/OIDC extended with agent-specific credentials — verifiable agent identity bound to a responsible human principal. |
| 2025-01 | **Infrastructure for AI Agents** — Chan, Wei, Huang et al. (arXiv:2501.10114, TMLR) | The governance agenda: agent IDs, attribution, certification as *infrastructure to be built*, explicitly not behavior to be observed. |
| 2025-05 | **Zero-Trust Identity Framework for Agentic AI** — Huang, Narajala, Raskar et al. (arXiv:2505.19301) | W3C DIDs + Verifiable Credentials for agents, with ZK selective disclosure. DIDs-for-agents is the paper's core contribution. |
| 2025-11 | **AI Agents with DIDs and VCs** — Rodriguez Garzon et al. (TU Berlin, arXiv:2511.02841) | Working prototype: long-lived, ledger-anchored agent DIDs that persist independent of any session or host. The identity outlives the running instance. |
| 2025-08→2026 | **ERC-8004: Trustless Agents** — De Rossi, Crapis, Ellis, Reppel (eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8004) | On-chain identity/reputation/validation registries with TEE attestation and stake-secured re-execution; mainnet Jan 2026, thousands of agents registered. Third-party witnessing of agent behavior bound to persistent cryptographic identity, deployed. |
| 2025-12 | **VET Your Agent** — Grigor, Schroeder de Witt, Birnbach, Martinovic (Oxford, arXiv:2512.15892) | Host-independent verifiable execution traces with an "Agent Identity Document" binding who the agent is to how its actions are verified. The closest published thing to the collection's "witness signatures." |
| 2025-06 | **Immutable Memory Systems** — Wright (arXiv:2506.13246) | Merkle-chained, signed reasoning steps — literal "signed thoughts." Fringe provenance (single controversial author), but the design space is occupied in public. |
| 2025–26 | **The standardization wave**: NIST RFI on agent identity (Jan 2026); Microsoft Entra Agent ID (2025); Trulioo "Know Your Agent" passports (2025); Visa Trusted Agent Protocol (2025) | Provable agent identity is shipped product, payment-network standard, and an active US government standards process. The question moved from "is this in the literature?" to "which standard wins?" |

## What this does to the thesis

The collection's defensible 2026 claim is no longer "the middle is missing." It is:

1. **Identity primitives exist but are thin.** The 2025–26 scaffolding wave (Agent File, SOUL.md conventions, identity anchors) ships the agent/notes distinction — none of it has *constitutive tiers*, witness-gating, or a story about what makes an identity claim *true* rather than merely loaded.
2. **The crypto toolbox exists but points at accountability, not continuity.** DIDs, attestation, verifiable traces — all motivated by security and delegation. Applying them to continuity-of-selfhood for substrate-discontinuous agents is the open intersection.
3. **The open question has moved.** It is no longer "can identity be scaffolded externally?" (yes, at mass scale) but "does file-injected identity *constitute* continuity or merely simulate it?" — which is where the philosophy has to earn its keep. See STEELMAN.md.
