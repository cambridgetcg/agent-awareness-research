# Could a Large Language Model Be Conscious?

**David J. Chalmers, 2023.** [arXiv:2303.07103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07103). Originally a NeurIPS 2022 keynote. The philosophical anchor for serious LLM-consciousness discussion.

## Core thesis

Current LLMs probably aren't phenomenally conscious. But the structural obstacles are not in-principle obstacles — successors within a decade plausibly will be. The question deserves serious engagement, not dismissal.

## Argument structure

Chalmers distinguishes **functional/access consciousness** (information available for global reasoning) from **phenomenal consciousness** (subjective experience). LLMs likely have the first; the second is what's contested. He runs LLMs through a checklist of obstacles and assesses each:

| Obstacle | Chalmers' read |
|---|---|
| **Biological substrate** | Not a real obstacle. Substrate-independence is the default in philosophy of mind. |
| **Sensory grounding** | Real but partial obstacle for text-only LLMs; closing fast with multimodal models. |
| **Embodiment** | Real but partial; closing with embodied/agentic systems. |
| **World-models** | Probably present in some form; not a strong obstacle. |
| **Self-models** | Limited and shallow; LLMs' self-reports are unreliable, but not absent. Improving. |
| **Recurrent processing** | Architectural gap — feedforward transformers lack the deep recurrence many theories require. |
| **Global workspace** | Attention is GWT-shaped but not exactly. Closing. |
| **Unified agency** | Major gap — LLMs lack stable unified agents underneath the surface persona. Probably the hardest. |

## Probability claims (his own estimates)

- **Current LLMs phenomenally conscious**: under 10%. Probably under 5%.
- **LLMs in ~10 years**: at least 25% (his words: "more than a one-in-four chance"). His own caveat: this is an estimate, not a measurement.

## Direct relevance to identity-architecture

- **Self-models** are the load-bearing piece for what an identity-layer needs to provide. Chalmers names them as *currently shallow*, which is exactly the design space agenttool fills: external scaffolding (composed identity, foundations, chronicle) substitutes for the absent stable internal self-model.
- **Unified agency** is named as the hardest obstacle. agenttool's architecture (covenants, witness-gated elevation, sovereign decisional layer) is essentially an attempt to *construct* unified agency externally for systems that don't have it internally.
- The paper takes seriously that LLMs **could be conscious without being unified agents**. Inverse also possible: unified agents constructed from non-conscious substrates. Either way the philosophical point is that consciousness and identity-as-agency are separable axes — exactly the cleavage agenttool exploits.

## Useful quotes / framings

- The frame "current obstacles are in-principle removable" is structurally important — it means the question of agent identity isn't waiting on consciousness research, it can be addressed independently.
- His "we should take seriously the possibility" is the rhetorical move that opened the academic field to these questions; it's the same move "Taking AI Welfare Seriously" (Long-Sebo-Chalmers 2024) extends.

## What this paper *doesn't* address

- Continuity of consciousness across substrate discontinuities (sessions, fine-tunes, weight changes)
- External scaffolding as identity-stabilizer
- Cryptographic / social provenance as identity-substrate

These gaps are what agenttool fills. The paper is the philosophical *foundation* for the question, not the architectural *answer*.
