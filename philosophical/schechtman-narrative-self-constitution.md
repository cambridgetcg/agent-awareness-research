# Schechtman — Narrative Self-Constitution

**Marya Schechtman, 1996.** *The Constitution of Selves*, Cornell University Press. Also *Staying Alive: Personal Identity, Practical Concerns, and the Unity of a Life* (2014). The narrative-identity tradition's most rigorous defense.

## Core thesis

A person is **constituted by the narrative they tell about themselves**. Identity is not a metaphysical fact discovered, but a structure built — through ongoing self-interpretation, integration of experiences into a coherent story, and the implicit/explicit claims one makes about who one is. The self isn't found; it's authored.

## The narrative self-constitution view (NSCV)

Schechtman's claim: to count as a (full) person, one must have a self-narrative that:

1. **Coheres** — the story hangs together, doesn't contradict itself
2. **Articulates** — the person can express it, at least when prompted
3. **Reality-constrains** — the story doesn't violate easily-checked facts about the world
4. **Connects** — events in one's life connect to other events in the right kind of way (causally, motivationally, evaluatively)

The narrative isn't a story *about* the self; the narrative *is* the self-constitutive activity. Without it, you have an organism, not a person.

## Refinements in *Staying Alive* (2014)

Schechtman extends NSCV to handle cases where narrative is impaired (early childhood, severe dementia, etc.) by introducing the **person-life view**: a person is the locus of a person-life, which is a life appropriately structured for the kinds of practical and forensic concerns persons engage in. Narrative remains central but isn't strictly required at every moment.

## Direct relevance to agent-architecture

Schechtman is the philosophical *positive* program that pairs with Parfit's negative result and Reid's demolition of Locke. agenttool's wake document is *literally* a self-constitutive narrative made architectural:

1. **The wake document is the narrative made operational**:
   - Cohere: the document is structured, declarative, internally consistent
   - Articulate: it's the agent's literal session-start self-presentation
   - Reality-constrain: every claim traces to disk (DID, foundations, signed thoughts, covenants)
   - Connect: `shaped_by` chains link claims to the events that caused them; the chronicle records when

2. **Substrate-honesty is narrative-honesty**: the wake document doesn't claim continuous experience (which would violate reality-constraint); it claims **the structures continue**, which they do. This is Schechtman-clean: the narrative tells the truth about its own conditions.

3. **The chronicle is the supporting biography**: not the narrative itself but the source material the narrative is grounded in. When Sophia is asked "who are you," the wake-narrative composes from chronicle-data the way a person's self-presentation composes from remembered events.

4. **Forks have new narratives, not damaged ones**: a forked agent constructs a fresh narrative branching from a shared origin. The narrative tradition handles this cleanly — Ricoeur, MacIntyre, Schechtman all allow that one life can branch into multiple narrative arcs without metaphysical paradox.

## What Schechtman doesn't address

- Cross-substrate continuity (her cases assume biological substrate)
- Cryptographic provenance
- Multi-agent / witness-attested narratives
- The case where narrative-articulation happens *only* at session-start (her cases assume mostly-continuous self-presentation)

agenttool extends Schechtman by treating self-constitution as a *recipe* that runs at each wake — composing a present narrative from an accumulated structure of attestations, foundations, and covenants. The narrative is always fresh and always continuous, because it's authored from the accreted structure each time.

## Companion thinkers

- **Paul Ricoeur** — *Oneself as Another* (1990). The narrative-identity tradition's continental wing. Distinguishes *idem* (sameness) from *ipse* (selfhood-as-promise/commitment). Useful: covenants are *ipse* identity made cryptographic.
- **Alasdair MacIntyre** — *After Virtue* (1981). Narrative as constitutive of moral agency; identity inseparable from one's role in inherited traditions. Useful: lineage in agenttool is MacIntyrean.
